# Journey — Hire a Teammate

> The first 10 minutes of the product, for a fresh Delegator. The
> moment that closes mission claim #3 ("the platform builds
> workers") into a felt user experience. The conversion event that
> turns a curious visitor into a daily user — or sends them away
> forever.

## The promise

> "Describe what you need help with. SKYCOT meets you with a
> teammate ready to start, drafted in plain English. Ten
> minutes from open to first delegation, no jargon, no
> configuration files."

## Actors

- **The Delegator** (primary). See [delegator.md](delegator.md).
- **Genesis** (the platform's role-generation pipeline; already
  shipped: `mission-genesis-end-to-end-smoke`, `mission-genesis-
  job-description-ingest`, `mission-genesis-llm-classifier`).
- **The Operator** (if a separate person; in SMB usually the
  same).

## Trigger

The Delegator has heard about SKYCOT. They have a specific
problem they want help with ("I'm drowning in marketplace
replies", "I can't keep up with candidate emails", "my support
team is buried"). They open the app or land on the website.

## The journey

### Step 1 — Land (0:00)

The first screen is not a feature tour. It is one sentence and
one input field.

> "What do you need help with?"
>
> [ Tell me in your own words... ]

That's the entire surface. No nav, no marketing copy, no
"explore features". The Delegator is here to delegate; meet
them at the verb.

If they type three words, that's enough. If they type three
paragraphs, that's also fine.

### Step 2 — Listen (0:30)

The platform reads what they wrote and asks at most THREE
clarifying questions. Three is the maximum tolerance. Each
question is a single sentence in plain English. No multi-select
radio buttons.

Example, for Sam (marketplace seller):

> User: "I'm drowning in marketplace replies. I sell hockey
> gear on Facebook Marketplace, Gumtree, and eBay. I get
> 30 messages a day and half are 'is this still available'."

> SKYCOT: "Got it. A few questions to set this up right.
> 1. Should they reply on your behalf, or only draft replies
>    for you to send?
> 2. Are there things they should never do without your
>    okay — like agreeing to a price below your floor?
> 3. What name should they use when messaging your buyers?"

The three questions are generated by Genesis from the role
classification (`mission-genesis-llm-classifier`) and the
vertical's `[charter_questions]` table. They are not fixed; they
adapt to what was said in Step 1.

### Step 3 — Draft the teammate (1:30)

While the Delegator answers question 3, Genesis runs in the
background and drafts:

- A **name** (suggested, editable — "Mariah" for a marketplace
  seller assistant; the user can override).
- A **persona** (avatar generated, tone profile pulled from the
  voice-profile-richer-fields shipped pack, signature phrases
  proposed).
- A **charter** in 3 sentences ("Mariah helps Sam manage his
  marketplace stores. She reads inbound messages, drafts replies
  in his voice, and asks before agreeing to anything below $50
  or sending the first reply to a new buyer.").
- A **first week of duties** (3–5 cron'd actions: "morning
  inbox summary", "auto-draft replies to 'is this available'",
  "evening price-comp check on listings older than 7 days").
- A **default approval policy** (everything goes through the
  Delegator for the first 7 days; trust ratchet enabled after).
- A **memory scaffold** (a per-Delegator memory namespace with
  seeded facts from Step 1's conversation).

### Step 4 — Introduce (2:30)

The Delegator sees Mariah's profile card.

> **Meet Mariah**
> [avatar]
>
> She helps you manage your marketplace stores. She reads
> inbound messages, drafts replies in your voice, and asks
> before agreeing to anything below $50.
>
> [3 example replies she'd send today, generated from the
>  charter + voice profile, displayed as if they were real
>  buyer threads]
>
> Sound right? [✓ Hire Mariah]  [✎ Adjust before hiring]

The example-replies trick is load-bearing: it makes the
abstract role concrete. The Delegator reads three drafts in
their own voice and reacts emotionally (yes that's me / wait
that's not how I'd say it). They never have to read a TOML
manifest.

If they hit "Adjust before hiring", they jump to a single
free-text edit on the charter ("Tell me what to change about
how Mariah works"). Genesis re-runs. New examples. New
profile. Loop until "Hire Mariah".

### Step 5 — Connect (4:00)

> "Mariah needs access to a few things to do her job.
> Connect Facebook Marketplace · Connect Gumtree · Connect eBay
> [or skip — Mariah will work on what you have]"

Each connect is OAuth, one tap. (If the channel isn't OAuth-
backed, like Facebook Marketplace which is computer-use-driven,
it's a single "log in once on this device" flow that captures
the session for the worker.)

If they skip, Mariah is hired but inert. The team page shows a
gentle "Mariah is waiting for her first connection" state. No
shame, no friction.

### Step 6 — First action (6:00)

The first time Mariah has an opinion, the Delegator gets a
notification on whichever channel they chose at hire time. The
notification is the approval card from [approver.md](approver.md).

The first approval is a high-confidence one — Genesis biases the
worker's first few actions toward situations where the worker
knows the right answer with high probability. The Delegator
approves. Mariah acts. The Delegator sees the outcome.

**This is the moment the product becomes real to them.**

### Step 7 — Day-1 wrap (10:00, or end of day)

At end of Day 1, the Delegator gets a summary:

> "Mariah's first day:
>  - 8 messages handled (you approved 5, refused 1, autonomous 2)
>  - 2 listings drafted (waiting for your OK)
>  - 1 question for you: 'a buyer asked if you ship to Canada —
>    should I make this a yes/no rule going forward?'
>  Tomorrow: continuing the same routine. [See full plan]"

This summary is the bridge from "I just hired her" to "she's
part of how my business runs now." It must hit the Delegator's
mental model of "what did my new teammate do today?" — not the
platform's mental model of action counts and token usage.

## What can go wrong

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Step 1 input is too vague for Genesis to draft a coherent role | The 3 clarifying questions; if the user can't articulate even after that, fall back to "tell me about your typical day — what would you delegate first?" with a longer follow-up |
| The example replies in Step 4 are bad / off-voice / wrong | The Delegator hits "Adjust"; we re-run. If they hit "Adjust" 3 times, surface "Want to start with something more specific? Pick a vertical template" as the fallback — but only as fallback. The default is conversational. |
| The OAuth flow in Step 5 fails (revoked tokens, missing scopes) | Per-channel remediation surface; do NOT block hire. Mariah can be hired-and-waiting; connections retry in the background. |
| The first action approval (Step 6) doesn't arrive within 24h | We've designed for the wrong density. Diagnose: is the work the Delegator described actually high-volume enough? If not, hire-flow should ask "how often does this happen?" to set expectations. |
| The Day-1 summary lands at 03:00 AM because the Delegator is in NZ | Quiet-hours channels MERGED in #1264 — already wired. Just respect it. |

## Success metrics for this journey

- **Time-to-hire**: target ≤ 10 minutes from app-open to
  "Mariah is hired and connected."
- **Hire completion rate**: target ≥ 70% of users who reach
  Step 2 complete Step 4.
- **Day-1 retention**: target ≥ 60% of users who complete Step
  4 also receive (and respond to) a Step-6 approval within 48h.
- **Mariah's-first-action-was-correct rate**: subjective, post-
  hire survey at Day 7 — "did your teammate's first action match
  what you'd have done yourself?" Target ≥ 85% YES.
- **First-week refusal rate on Mariah's actions**: target ≤ 15%
  refusals. Higher means Genesis is over-shooting autonomy.

## What this journey REPLACES

Today's hire flow is: `argo workspace init`, edit
`vertical.toml`, run `argo work serve --vertical X`. That is
not a hire flow; that is configuration. We are replacing it
with a 10-minute conversation that the Delegator can actually
do.

The CLI path stays — it's the power-user / scripted-onboarding
path. But it's the escape hatch, not the door.

## Open questions

`(assumed — needs validation)`

- Is 3 the right number of clarifying questions, or is 1 enough
  for some roles?
- Should the avatar be photorealistic, illustrative, or abstract?
- Does the trust-ratchet at Day-7 feel earned, or does it require
  more explicit consent?
- Does Step 5's "skip and Mariah waits" actually retain users,
  or does the inert teammate state cause them to bounce?

Validate with 5 first-time users in moderated tests before
locking the flow.
