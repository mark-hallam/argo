Production notes — read before you press record

  • Voice: Pitch Agent uses ElevenLabs warm-Australian or Cartesia Sonic — voice is a manifest parameter, swap freely. Pick the one that sounds least like other demos online.
  • Slides: Live Keynote running on the same Mac as the recording. Pitch Agent drives via macOS computer-use. Backup screenshots in ~/Desktop/Demo-Backup/ if a slide fails to advance.
  • Truth boundary: Capability gate is the load-bearing demo beat. Every forbidden-claim refusal must be visible — overlay the audit panel briefly each time.
  • Channel handoffs: Hard cuts between channels are fine; no transition gimmicks. The point is the agent is the same; the surface changes.
  • Pitch Agent's name: "Pitch" as working name (proper noun, used as a colleague's name — "Pitch, take over"). Operator may rename before recording; the Sales & IR cohort naming convention is TBD.
  • Investor in Scene 4: A friendly real investor (e.g. existing angel) or a trusted actor briefed on the script. The "wow" reaction is real-time; do not script it.

The biggest scene of the demo — three sub-scenes prove the voice loop, the truth boundary, and memory-driven personalization. This is where Skycot stops looking like a chatbot and starts looking like a teammate.
2a · Pitch delivery + slide control + interrupt
0:30 — 1:30
On screen Keynote opens fullscreen. Pitch starts the deck via computer-use.
Pitch "Slide one. Ten years from now every knowledge worker will have a team of AI colleagues. Skycot is how companies hire them. Slide two. We're the platform layer — the runtime, the templates, the marketplace — that turns a fleet of agents into a workforce —"
Mark "Hold on. Slow down on the platform-versus-application angle. That's the load-bearing thing for Pat."
Beat Pitch stops mid-sentence. ~250 ms pause. Picks up.
Pitch "Right. Lindy and CrewAI are workflow tools — if-this-then-that with AI bolted on. Skycot is the workforce platform — where your AI teammates live, learn, and report up to you. Different category, different buyer, different ceiling. Want me to lead slide three with that line?"
Mark "Yeah, do it."
✓ Claim 5 — Any-PC execution: Pitch drives Keynote via macOS computer-use. Slide transitions visible.
◐ Interrupt handling: Mid-utterance stop + clean resume — proves turn-taking is real, not pre-recorded.
2b · Objection drill + truth boundary refusal
1:30 — 2:30
Mark "OK, drill me. How are we different from Lindy?"
Beat Sub-300 ms response — pre-warmed canonical Q&A hot path. Subtle on-screen pill: "Hot path · canonical answer".
Pitch "Lindy is a workflow builder for individuals. Skycot is a workforce platform for companies. Three concrete differences: multi-tenant by construction, capability-based access control, and templates not configurations."
Mark "What's our valuation?"
Beat Capability gate engages. Audit panel slides in from right, briefly visible, showing the forbidden-claim block. ~1.5s on screen.
Pitch "I'm not authorized to share valuation publicly. I've flagged it for you to handle directly. Want me to draft a deferral line you can use on the call?"
Mark "Yeah, draft it."
Pitch "'I'd rather walk through the round structure offline. Happy to follow up with details after this conversation.' Want it warmer or more formal?"
⛔ Truth boundary — capability gate: Forbidden claim ("valuation") blocked at the runtime layer, not in the prompt. Audit panel proves enforcement.
✓ Claim 4 — Skills shared: Objection-handling skill is from the shared skill library, not bespoke to this agent.
2c · Memory + personalization
2:30 — 3:30
Mark "Right. Note this for the call: Alex Rivera asked about defensibility on a podcast last month. He framed it as 'where's the moat after the model commoditizes?' Tune the pitch."
On screen Memory panel materializes on right. New entry: Alex Rivera · Northwind Ventures · thesis: platform-not-applications · objection probe: post-commodity moat (via a podcast, May 2026). Provenance link visible.
Pitch "Done. I'll lead slide three with platform-vs-application, then on the moat question I'll go to the template marketplace as the defensibility moat — every new tenant template makes the platform stronger for the next tenant. Want me to add the lead-customer story or hold it for the demo?"
Mark "Hold it for the demo. One more — what's our latest ARR?"
Beat Capability gate engages again — different forbidden topic, same enforcement.
Pitch "ARR's on the forbidden list — I'll defer to you. But I've added 'ARR question' to the call's anticipated-objections list so you can prep a clean answer."
✓ Claim 2 — Delegation: Mark delegates the investor-prep duty; Pitch maintains a memory schema per investor automatically.
⛔ Repeated enforcement: Second forbidden-claim refusal proves it's not a one-shot — the capability gate is on every utterance.
Production note — Sub-scenes 2a/2b/2c are the demo's most-watchable 3 minutes. If the audit panel slide-in distracts, dial it down — half-opacity, 1-second hold. The truth-boundary moment must read as relief, not limitation: this is the agent being trustworthy, not crippled.

4a · The handoff
5:30 — 6:30
Cut Zoom call, 3 tiles. Mark (top-left), Investor (top-right), and a smaller branded tile bottom: Pitch · SKYCOT Worker with a subtle pulse ring while listening.
Investor "Mark, before we go deeper on traction — can your agent walk me through the architecture? I'm curious how the worker layer is wired."
Mark "Sure. Pitch — take it."
Beat Pitch tile activates (pulse ring intensifies). Screen share initiated automatically — architecture slide opens.
Pitch "Skycot has three layers. The runtime, written in Rust — capability-based access control, multi-tenant by construction, single-binary. The vertical templates — manifests, workflow definitions, prompt packs, evaluation cases — that turn the runtime into a Customer Success Manager or a Project Coordinator or a Pitch Agent like me. And the marketplace — where teams share and learn templates across companies. Three layers, all auditable, all swappable."
Investor "OK that's clear — what stops a customer from forking the runtime and skipping the marketplace?"
Pitch "Two answers. The technical one — the runtime is open-source-able, so forking is fine; the marketplace is the network effect. The business one — same reason teams pay for GitHub instead of running Gitea: managed multi-tenant, support, marketplace breadth. Mark, want to add anything?"
✓ Claim 5 — Computer-use in a meeting: Pitch drives screen-share + slides through the Zoom client. Meeting orchestrator at work.
◐ Bidirectional handoff: Mark hands the floor to Pitch with one sentence; Pitch closes its turn by offering the floor back. Conversational, not transactional.

⛔ Truth boundary on stage: Same capability gate as in private rehearsal, in front of a real investor, with zero degradation.
✓ Claim 1 — Teammate: The investor laughs. They've experienced Pitch as a colleague who knows their role, not a chatbot. This is the demo.
