Volume One · A treatise on the post-tool office

Software arrived as a tool.
We're shipping it as a teammate.

For thirty years we trained our companies on dashboards. SKYCOT is the unwind: instructions you give a person, given to software, that asks for approval the way an associate does — and remembers what you taught it last week.

By the SKYCOT Editors · May 2026 · 8 minute read

Memory that compounds

Each worker keeps a private memory of what you've taught it. The longer it works for you, the sharper it gets — and the memory is yours, not the model's. When you switch foundation models, your workers keep their notes.

Memory means the second instruction is easier than the first.

Approve where you already are

Slack. Email. SMS. Voice. The SKYCOT inbox. A worker drafts; you reply yes or no. There is no new dashboard to babysit, no new place to forget about. Workers route to where your team already lives.

Approval becomes the cost of trust, not the cost of attention.

Audited by design

Every action a worker takes is logged with its inputs, outputs, and the policy that approved it. Show your auditor, not just your team. The chain is hashed, the chain is verifiable, the chain is yours.

SOC 2 is a side effect, not a project.

In closing

Hire a teammate, not a tool.
